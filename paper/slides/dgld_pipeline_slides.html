DGLD Domain-Gated Latent Diffusion

How we generate a new explosive, end to end

A diffusion model, a generator that turns random noise into structure step by step, proposes candidate molecules; first-principles physics decides which ones are real. This deck walks the whole pipeline: the data, the four training stages, sampling, and the filter funnel that removes everything the surrogate, the fast property predictor standing in for real physics, only thought was good.

Setup/01 The target

What counts as a good answer

WhyBefore judging any method, be explicit about what a useful molecule has to satisfy. Four requirements, and they pull against each other.
1 · Performance

Release a lot of energy, very fast. Measured as detonation velocity D and pressure P, both driven mostly by crystal density. Target: the HMX / PETN class, today’s benchmark high explosives.

2 · Stability

Do not go off when dropped, struck or warmed. Measured as impact sensitivity h50, plus thermal stability and the absence of known-dangerous groups.

3 · Synthesis

Somebody has to be able to make it, from starting materials that exist, in a sane number of steps. A molecule nobody can build is a drawing.

4 · Novelty

It must be genuinely new: not in the chemical databases, not in the patent literature, not a re-run of the training set. Otherwise it is a lookup, not a discovery.

The difficulty is that these conflict. The usual way to raise energy release is to pack in more nitro groups (oxygen-rich –NO2 units) and strain, which is also the usual way to make a molecule dangerously sensitive. Anyone can win on one axis alone. The search is for a molecule that holds all four at once, and every stage that follows exists to enforce one of them.

Setup/02 The problem

The data is uneven in three separate ways

The corpus is assembled from several public sources that differ in both reliability and chemical scope. Nothing about it is uniform, and each kind of unevenness needs its own answer.

1 · Chemistry

Not everything is an explosive, and that is deliberate. Broad chemistry does three jobs: a checkpoint pretrained on general molecules teaches the encoder what a molecule even is; a ~380 k unlabelled CHNO (carbon, hydrogen, nitrogen and oxygen only) dump teaches the prior what plausible chemistry looks like; drug-like molecules act as negatives.

2 · Coverage

Few rows have all four properties. One molecule may have a measured density but no heat of formation. Each row carries its own list of which properties are actually present.

3 · Provenance

A number is not just a number. Some were measured, some computed, some produced by a formula, some guessed by a model. They are not interchangeable.

Total molecules seen in training
~694,000

66k labelled + 380k unlabelled CHNO + motif-augmented (grafted-on energetic groups), after dedup

Molecules with reported properties
~66,000

of those, the ones carrying detonation numbers

Whose numbers you can trust
~3,000

measured in a lab, or computed with quantum mechanics

The other 95 % are estimates, and the estimates are not equally good either. A model trained on all of it imitates the estimates, and proposes molecules that collapse once real physics is applied.

Setup/03 The core idea

Sort every label by how much you trust it

Unevenness 3 gets the architectural answer: every individual label is tagged with how it was produced. Unevenness 2 is handled by the same mechanism, since a row simply carries no tag for a property it lacks.

Tier AExperimental Somebody made the compound and measured it in a lab.
Tier BDFT Computed from quantum mechanics: no lab, but no guessing either.
Tier CKamlet–Jacobs A 1968 formula. Instant, but unreliable outside the chemistry it was fitted on.
Tier DML surrogatePredicted by the Uni-Mol 3D ensemble. A guess about a guess.

Tier C is the Kamlet–Jacobs formula, a fixed equation with no learned parameters. Tier D is Uni-Mol, a neural network: pretrained on millions of molecules in 3D, so it judges a candidate by its shape in space rather than by its formula. Ours is an in-house ensemble fine-tuned on detonation data (~84 M parameters per fold, two folds). It answers in milliseconds where DFT takes an hour, which is why it can label the whole corpus, and why its answers are the least trusted tier.

Uni-Mol: Zhou, G. et al. (2023), Uni-Mol: A Universal 3D Molecular Representation Learning Framework, ICLR 2023. Kamlet & Jacobs (1968), J. Chem. Phys. 48, 23–35.

The gate: only Tier A/B may steer generation toward a property target. Tier C/D still train the model on what a plausible molecule looks like. They shape the background, they never give orders.

Setup/04 Adapting Uni-Mol

We do not train Uni-Mol from scratch. We adapt it.

WhyPretraining a 3D molecular model needs millions of molecules and a budget we do not have. Somebody already did it, on general chemistry. We only have to teach it our question.
1Take the public Uni-Mol checkpoint, pretrained on millions of molecules in 3D (Zhou et al., ICLR 2023). Not ours, not energetic-specific.existing model
2Build a 3D shape for each molecule first: the text has no coordinates, so RDKit generates a conformer (ETKDGv3) and relaxes it with the MMFF94 force field This is why Uni-Mol is a label-maker and not a per-step guide: doing it 40 times per molecule would be ruinous.conformer
3Fine-tune it on our labelled energetic data, as a regression model over eight outputs ρ, D, P, T, E, V, HOF, BDEour data
4Ensemble two folds and keep both, to damp single-model error2 × ~84 M

Trained on tiers A + B + C only. That is deliberate: it learns from experimental, DFT and formula-derived rows, then produces the Tier D values for molecules that have nothing. Training it on its own output would be circular. Held-out quality is R² = 0.84 to 0.92 in 5-fold cross-validation, good enough to rank candidates and not good enough to trust as a final answer, which is precisely why Tier D sits at the bottom and why the DFT stage exists.

Setup/05 Terminology

Two different things are called “labels”

WhyThe word is overloaded in this field, and mixing the two up makes the whole architecture look arbitrary. They come from different places and do different jobs.
Property targets

The four detonation numbers: ρ, D, P, HOF (density, velocity, pressure, heat of formation).

Where from: the dataset. Measured, computed or estimated, which is exactly what the trust tiers rank.

Used for: conditioning the denoiser (the network that generates molecules). They are the “what to aim for”, gated by the mask, a per-molecule on/off switch.

Coverage: patchy. Many molecules are missing some.

Quality signals

Viability, sensitivity, hazard (plus SA and SC, two drug-domain scores for how hard a molecule looks to make).

Where from: nowhere. Nobody reports them, so we manufacture them for every molecule from rules, correlations and a classifier.

Used for: training the score model (the judge that can read a half-finished molecule), which then steers sampling. Never conditioning.

Coverage: complete, by construction.

Keep the split in mind for the rest of the deck: property targets are conditioned on; quality signals are steered by. The mask governs the first group only, which is why it has exactly four bits.

Setup/06 The two computed tiers

How DFT and Kamlet–Jacobs actually work

Tiers B and C are both computed rather than measured, but they are not the same kind of computation, and they run in sequence.

DFT
structure → ρ, HOF
Density functional theory. A quantum-mechanics calculation of a molecule’s shape and energy from the behaviour of its electrons. Takes only the structure and returns optimised geometry and electronic energy, giving density ρ and heat of formation HOF. No experimental input, hence first principles. About an hour of GPU time per molecule: accurate, far too slow for thousands of candidates.
Kamlet–Jacobs
ρ, HOF → D, P
A closed-form formula from 1968. Takes the density and heat of formation from DFT plus the atom counts, and returns detonation velocity D and pressure P instantly. Being an empirical fit, it drifts outside the chemistry it was built on, notably for nitrogen-rich molecules, exactly the region we are searching.

structure  →  DFT  →  ρ, HOF  →  Kamlet–Jacobs  →  D, P. Neither step is measured; each adds its own error.

Setup/07 Design logic

Why the pipeline has the shape it has

WhyEvery stage exists because a specific obstacle stands between “we want a molecule” and “here is one worth making”. Nothing in the design is decorative.
The obstacleWhat it forcesStage
Molecules are discrete text. Diffusion only works on numbers that vary smoothly. Learn a numeric space where similar molecules sit close together. Encoder
The properties we want to aim at are mostly untrustworthy estimates. Rank each label by provenance, and let only reliable ones steer. Trust tiers
Nobody publishes “is this viable” or “is this safe”. Manufacture those signals for every molecule in the corpus. Labels
Mid-generation there is no molecule yet, only a noisy vector. Ordinary chemistry tools cannot read it. Train a judge that works on the half-formed state. Score model
Something has to actually invent structures, not just rate them. Train a generator, conditioned on the targets. Denoiser
One run explores one region and stops. Run several differently-tuned lanes and pool the results. Sampling
A model's own score is an opinion. Surrogates can be gamed, and they were. Re-test every survivor with real physics before reporting it. Funnel

Encoder/09 Training 1 of 4

Compress every molecule into a vector

WhyDiffusion works by nudging numbers, so it needs a space where similar molecules sit close together. Chemical text has no usable sense of “nearby”, so we build that space first.

Molecules are written as SELFIES strings, a notation where every string is a valid molecule. A variational autoencoder (LIMO) learns to squeeze each one into 1024 numbers and rebuild it.

Input

O=[N+]([O−])c1noc…

Latent vector

[0.42, −1.10, …]
1024 dimensions

Rebuilt

O=[N+]([O−])c1noc…

Trained once, then frozen, and every latent is cached. From here on the pipeline is pure vector maths, which is what makes diffusion practical on a small GPU budget.

“Energetic-biased” means the corpus is deliberately skewed toward explosive chemistry, not general molecules. It is built from three pools: the 66k labelled master, a ~380k unlabelled CHNO dump from the same domain, and a large motif-augmented expansion that programmatically grafts nitro, nitramine, azide, cyano, tetrazole and oxadiazole groups onto known energetic scaffolds (core molecular skeletons). The encoder therefore spends its fine-tuning budget on the chemistry we care about instead of on drug-like molecules.

MLP-VAE, 1024-d latent, ~34 M parameters. Starts from the published LIMO checkpoint trained on general chemistry, then fine-tuned ~8,500 steps on 326 k energetic-biased SMILES, the standard text notation for a structure.

Labels/10 Derive, learn, steer

Three jobs, and only the last one touches generation

WhyTo steer generation toward good chemistry, we need a number that says what good means. The corpus does not carry one, so we have to create it.

The molecules are real; most of the labels are manufactured. Nobody publishes a molecule’s “viability” or “hazard” score, so we derive those from domain knowledge, physics correlations and formulas.

1Derive a label for every molecule in the corpus, once, before training startsoffline
2Learn one model that reproduces those labels from a half-noised moleculethe score model
3Steer generation with that learned model, once at every sampling stepat sampling

The generators below never steer anything. They exist to create the training signal for step 2.

Labels/12 The toolbox, part 1

Tools that describe a molecule

Two of these describe a molecule going in to a classifier.

A fingerprint is not a label. A Morgan fingerprint only says which substructures are present; it has no opinion about whether the molecule is a good energetic material. The Random Forest is the function that turns those bits into a number. Fingerprint goes in, viability score comes out. You need both.

Morgan fingerprint
input → viability
Represents: which substructures a molecule contains. Turns a molecule into a fixed list of 1s and 0s: every small circular patch of atoms and neighbours is hashed into a slot. Molecules that share substructures light up the same slots, which is what lets a classifier compare them.
RDKit descriptors
input → viability
Represents: bulk make-up, size and composition. RDKit is the standard open-source chemistry toolkit; its descriptors are plain computed numbers such as molecular weight, ring count, number of nitro groups, oxygen balance, the last being whether a molecule carries enough oxygen to burn itself completely.

Labels/13 The toolbox, part 2

Tools that produce a value

The rest produce a value coming out: a number, or a yes/no class.

Politzer–Murray
predicts → sensitivity
Represents: how weak the weakest bond is. Published correlations linking the strength of a molecule’s weakest bond to how easily it detonates on impact. A weaker trigger bond means a more sensitive explosive.
h50
the sensitivity property itself
Represents: measured impact sensitivity, in centimetres. Drop a standard weight from increasing heights; h50 is the height in cm at which half the drops cause detonation. Higher is safer: TATB, an unusually insensitive explosive, sits near 490 cm, primary explosives at a few cm.
SMARTS + Bruns–Watson
decides → hazard
Represents: presence of a known-dangerous group. A yes/no class, not a number. SMARTS is a pattern language for substructures, regular expressions but for chemistry. Our hand-written catalogue is extended with the published Bruns–Watson demerit list, which penalises groups known to be reactive or unstable.
Uni-Mol ensemble
predicts → performance
Represents: the four target numbers ρ, D, P and HOF. Uni-Mol is a pretrained model that reads molecules in 3D. An in-house ensemble, fine-tuned on detonation data, returns all four in milliseconds instead of an hour, fast enough to score every candidate. Two folds of roughly 84 M parameters each. That speed is why it screens, and never decides.

Steering/14 A trick worth naming

Why train heads you will never use?

Some of the score model’s heads, the small output layers stacked on one shared trunk, never steer anything; they exist only during training. That sounds wasteful. It is the opposite.

The problem

Only ~3,000 trustworthy labels. A network with one job on a small dataset memorises it.

The fix

Give the shared trunk several related jobs at once. Each extra task constrains the representation the others depend on.

shared trunk · ~8 M parametersalmost all of the model lives here
↓   ↓   ↓   ↓   ↓   ↓
Viabilitysteers
Sensitivitysteers
Hazardsteers
Performancetrains only
SAtrains only
SCtrains only
Performance
Predicts the four detonation numbers (ρ, D, P, HOF). Deliberately kept off the steering bus: property targets are pursued through conditioning instead.
SA
Synthetic Accessibility. A drug-domain score for how hard a molecule looks to make. Calibrated on pharmaceutical reaction corpora, so it penalises the polynitro chemistry we want; production sets its steering scale to zero.
SC
Synthetic Complexity. A learned complexity score from a reaction corpus. Never wired into sample-time steering at all.

This is multi-task regularisation. The extra jobs are not there for their own answers: they force the trunk to encode general chemistry rather than a shortcut that satisfies one objective. The heads we use then read from a better trunk.

Each head is roughly 256k parameters against a ~8 M trunk, so what the six heads share is most of the model. Once training ends the three auxiliary outputs are discarded. What survives is the trunk.

Steering/15 Training 3 of 4

One trunk, six heads, trained together

WhyMid-generation there is no finished molecule to inspect, only a noisy vector. We need a judge that can read that half-formed state and still say something useful.

The score model judges a noisy latent, which matters, because at generation time everything is noisy. All six heads share a single trunk and are trained jointly, not separately.

4-block FiLM-MLP (FiLM: side information enters as a per-layer scale and shift), shared 1024-d trunk, ~8 M parameters, six heads at ~256k each.

Three heads steer

Viability ↑  Sensitivity ↓  Hazard ↓

Each has an independent on/off switch at sample time.

Three heads regularise

Performance, SA, SC

Never used for steering. They exist to improve the shared representation.

Self-distillation: let the model generate, catch the molecules that game the surrogate, feed them back as hard negatives. Two rounds, 918 hard negatives, refining the viability head.

Steering/16 The distinction that trips people up

Labelling and steering are different jobs

The Random Forest and Uni-Mol never steer generation. They run once, offline, to manufacture training labels. Only the score model is in the sampling loop.

WhenOn what dataWho runsHow often
Labelling
before training		 the 66k cached corpus Random Forest, Uni-Mol ensemble, SMARTS catalogue, Politzer–Murray fit once, offline

at sample time		 the half-noised molecule being built score model only every one of 40 steps

Why it has to work this way: the Random Forest and Uni-Mol can only read a finished molecule, and mid-diffusion there is no molecule yet, only a noisy vector. Their knowledge is transferred once into the score model, which steers on their behalf.

Generator/17 Training 4 of 4 · the generator

The denoiser, and how it learns

WhyEverything so far only describes molecules. This is the part that invents them.

This is the network that actually invents molecules: a 44.6 M parameter FiLM-ResNet working entirely on 1024-number latents. Training it is a fill-in-the-blank exercise.

1Take a real molecule from the corpus, as its cached latentz0
2Add a random amount of noise to itzt, t ∈ 1..1000
3Also hand it the property targets and the mask saying which of them countc, m
4Ask it to predict the noise that was added. That is the whole lossε-prediction

The properties enter through FiLM: the four numbers are turned into per-layer scale and shift factors applied inside the network, so the conditioning reaches every block rather than being concatenated once at the input.

The trick that makes both modes possible: 10 % of the time the whole mask is zeroed during training, so the same network also learns to denoise with no targets at all. One set of weights, two behaviours, which is exactly what classifier-free guidance, steering by the gap between a conditioned and an unconditioned run, needs at sample time.

1000-step variance-preserving DDPM (denoising diffusion probabilistic model) in latent space. Two are trained, DGLD-H and DGLD-P, giving the guided lanes their diversity.

Generator/18 Training 4 of 4 · the mechanism

The mask is where trust gating actually happens

WhyTwo awkward facts have to enter training: most rows lack some properties, and the ones they have differ in trust. The mask handles both at once.

At every training step, a four-bit switch decides which properties are allowed to guide this molecule.

ρ density  1 D velocity  0 P pressure  1 HOF  0
1Pick how many properties to use0–4
2Pick which, weighted so trusted labels are chosen more oftentier-weighted
3Randomly switch some off againp = 0.30
4Sometimes zero the whole mask → no steering at allp = 0.10

An all-zero mask is the unconditional branch, and that is exactly where the untrustworthy Tier C/D labels do their work. They teach plausibility, never targets.

Careful: two separate switches

The mask covers only the four property targets (ρ, D, P, HOF), which reach the denoiser as conditioning. The steering heads (viability, sensitivity, hazard) are not masked: they have their own scales, set once at sample time, and push on chemistry quality. The score model’s performance head is deliberately left off the steering bus, so property targets are pursued through conditioning alone.

Sampling/19 Inference · the two guidances

Noise in, molecule out: in 40 steps

WhyTraining is finished. Now we ask the model for molecules it has never seen, and steer it while it works.
1Start from pure random noise in latent spacezT ~ N(0, I)
2Denoise step by step (a DDIM sampler does it in 40 passes, not 1000), with two forces pushing at each step40 DDIM steps
3Decode the final vector back to a moleculefrozen decoder
Guidance 1 · classifier-free (CFG)

Steers toward the four property targets (ρ, D, P, HOF).

The denoiser was trained both with and without the targets, so at sample time we run it twice and amplify the difference. No separate predictor is needed.

scale w = 7

Guidance 2 · classifier (score model)

Steers on chemistry quality: viability up, sensitivity down, hazard down.

Uses a separately trained network that can read a half-noised molecule, and adds its gradient at every step. Each head has its own scale.

sviab 1.0 · ssens 0.3 · shaz 1.0

Why both? They answer different questions. CFG asks “hit these numbers”; the score model asks “be a sane, safe molecule”. Targets alone would happily produce something that scores well and cannot exist.

Sampling/20 Inference · lanes and merge

What a “lane” is, and what gets merged

WhyA single run explores one region and stops. To cover more chemistry we run several, then combine what they found.

A lane is one complete sampling run with its settings fixed throughout: (which denoiser, which property targets, which guidance). Run it and you get a list of molecules.

→draw noise → 40 guided steps → decodeone lane

H and P are not guidance signals; they are two denoisers. Both receive all four property targets and run at identical guidance settings. They differ only in how they were trained: each oversampled a different extreme, so DGLD-H reliably supplies high heat-of-formation leads and DGLD-P supplies high-density ones. Four lanes run with different settings, so each explores a different part of chemical space:

What is merged: the molecule lists, not the models. Each lane finishes and produces plain SMILES text; the lists are pooled and de-duplicated by canonical SMILES, so a molecule found twice is kept once. That pool enters the filter funnel. Nothing is averaged, and no weights are combined.

Validation/23 Synthesis routes

Can anyone actually make it?

WhyA molecule nobody can build is a drawing. But the tools that answer this question were built for pharmaceuticals, so a negative answer has to be read carefully.

Retrosynthesis is run with AiZynthFinder, which searches backwards from the target for a sequence of known reactions ending in purchasable starting materials. It uses public USPTO reaction templates, drawn from US patent filings, and the ZINC in-stock catalogue.

L1: route found

9 productive routes. The best is 4 steps of standard synthetic chemistry: ring nitration, Boc protection, then a DPPA-mediated Curtius rearrangement.

Hazard noted honestly: the acyl-azide intermediate is primary-explosive class and needs careful handling.

The other ten: none found

Zero productive routes within budget, and still zero at 5× budget on L4 and L5.

The energetic intermediates come back in_stock: false against ZINC, which is a catalogue of drug-discovery reagents.

What the 1-of-11 rate does and does not mean. USPTO templates and the ZINC catalogue are both drug-domain resources; nitration and nitrolysis chemistry, the reactions that install nitro groups, is thinly represented in them. So this measures a gap in the public template library, not proof that ten molecules are unmakeable. The paper claims only L1 as synthesis-actionable and reports the rest as a scaffold-diversity result.

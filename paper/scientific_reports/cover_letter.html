Dear Editors,

Please find enclosed our manuscript, “Domain-Gated Latent Diffusion: Generative Inverse Design of HMX-Class Energetic Materials with First-Principles Validation,” which we submit for consideration as an original research Article in the Guest-Edited Collection “Generative Modeling for Chemistry Discovery.”

Energetic materials power mining, demolition, propulsion, and automotive airbags, yet the compounds in service today were designed decades ago. Finding a successor means searching an enormous molecular space under conflicting demands: high energy release, low sensitivity to accidental initiation, and a practical synthesis route. Generative models are the natural search tool, but they fail here for a specific reason: the training data is mostly untrustworthy. Of roughly 66,000 molecules with recorded properties, only about 3,000 were measured experimentally or computed from first principles; models trained on all of them learn to imitate the rough estimates and propose candidates that collapse once real physics is applied.

We introduce Domain-Gated Latent Diffusion (DGLD), a diffusion model that treats data reliability as an explicit design parameter. A four-tier trust gate lets only reliable labels steer generation, while the unreliable majority still teaches the model what a plausible molecule looks like; a multi-task score model provides independently switchable control over viability, sensitivity, and hazard; and a four-stage screening funnel (SMARTS filtering, a Pareto reranker, GFN2-xTB triage, and two-level density functional theory) audits every candidate before it is reported.

Fit to the Collection. The manuscript speaks to all three of the Collection's stated themes. It contributes an algorithmic innovation in deep generative modelling, namely a diffusion prior that consumes unevenly reliable property labels by construction rather than by filtering them away. It is built around integration with quantum-chemical simulation, with DFT acting as a validation stage inside the discovery loop rather than as a post-hoc check, so that no candidate is reported until first-principles physics has been applied to it. And it is an application to advanced materials, on a target whose design objectives are in direct conflict. Because the label-trust gate is independent of chemistry, transferring to any inverse-design setting in which abundant weak labels surround a small reliable core, we expect the work to interest readers in catalysis and energy storage, where the same label-quality asymmetry arises, as well as the journal's broader machine-learning and materials readership.

The principal findings are:

  • DGLD proposes ten unique molecules that survive the full DFT audit, none of them present in PubChem, the SureChEMBL patent corpus, or the 65,980-row training set.
  • The best of them, 3,4,5-trinitro-1,2-isoxazole, matches the benchmark explosives HMX and PETN on calculated detonation performance (calibrated density 2.09 g cm−3, Kamlet–Jacobs detonation velocity 8.25 km s−1), is structurally unlike anything in the training set (nearest-neighbour Tanimoto 0.27), and comes with a four-step synthesis route, making it directly actionable for an experimental campaign.
  • Against four generative baselines on the same corpus, DGLD is the only method whose novel candidates remain on-target once first-principles physics is applied; the sole baseline whose best novel candidate was carried to DFT collapsed from 9.73 to 6.28 km s−1.
  • Because trust gating is independent of chemistry, the recipe transfers to any inverse-design setting in which abundant weak labels surround a small reliable core.

To support reproducibility, the code, trained model checkpoints, and datasets are released openly as a single archived package on Zenodo (DOI 10.5281/zenodo.19821952), and the full computational protocol is given in the Supplementary Information.

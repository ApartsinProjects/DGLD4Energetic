Dear Editors,

Please find enclosed our manuscript, “Domain-Gated Latent Diffusion: Generative Inverse Design of HMX-Class Energetic Materials with First-Principles Validation,” which we submit for consideration as an original research Article in the Guest-Edited Collection “Generative Modeling for Chemistry Discovery.”

The design of new high-energy-density materials is a data-limited inverse-design problem: of roughly 66,000 labelled CHNO molecules, only about 3,000 carry experimental or DFT-quality property values, and no new HMX-class compound has been disclosed in decades. We introduce Domain-Gated Latent Diffusion (DGLD), a generative diffusion framework that couples inverse design to first-principles validation. A four-tier label-trust gate routes only high-quality labels into the conditional gradient while noisy labels train the unconditional prior; a multi-task score model provides independently switchable steering over viability, sensitivity, and hazard; and a four-stage screening funnel (SMARTS filtering, a Pareto reranker, GFN2-xTB triage, and two-level density functional theory) validates every candidate.

Fit to the Collection and to Scientific Reports. The work is a diffusion-model architecture for molecular design, integrated end to end with quantum-chemical simulation, and applied to advanced energetic materials; it therefore addresses the Collection's stated focus on algorithmic innovations, integration with quantum-chemical workflows, and applications to advanced materials. Because the label-trust gating recipe is domain-agnostic, transferring to any inverse-design setting that pairs abundant weak labels with a scarce trustworthy subset, we expect the manuscript to interest the journal's broad readership in machine learning, computational chemistry, and materials science beyond the energetic-materials community.

The principal findings are:

  • DGLD yields ten unique DFT-confirmed leads in the HMX performance class, novel against PubChem, the SureChEMBL patent corpus, and the 65,980-row training master.
  • The headline compound, 3,4,5-trinitro-1,2-isoxazole, reaches a calibrated density of 2.09 g cm−3 and a Kamlet–Jacobs detonation velocity of 8.25 km s−1 while remaining structurally distinct from all 65,980 training molecules (nearest-neighbour Tanimoto 0.27).
  • Benchmarked against four generative baselines on the same corpus, DGLD is the only method with productive-quadrant coverage (novel and on-target) confirmed at the DFT level; the sole baseline whose best novel candidate was carried to DFT collapsed from 9.73 to 6.28 km s−1 under first-principles scoring.
  • The label-gating recipe is domain-agnostic and transfers to any inverse-design setting that pairs abundant weak labels with a scarce trustworthy subset and a suitable validation funnel.

To support reproducibility, the code, trained model checkpoints, and datasets are released openly on Zenodo (DOI 10.5281/zenodo.19821952) and mirrored on GitHub, and the full computational protocol is given in the Supplementary Information.

We are writing to ask whether our manuscript, “Domain-Gated Latent Diffusion for the Inverse Design of Novel HMX-Class Energetic Materials with First-Principles Validation,” would be a suitable contribution to the Special Issue “AI in Materials Design and Discovery” in Molecules, for which you serve as Guest Editor.

The work sits squarely at the intersection of the themes the Special Issue highlights: generative inverse design of molecules, data-driven discovery from reliability-stratified data, high-throughput screening, and the integration of first-principles calculations with machine learning. It addresses a central obstacle in data-sparse materials research: how to exploit a large but reliability-stratified molecular corpus without allowing lower-confidence estimates to distort the search for candidates with targeted physical properties. Our method unifies reliability-aware (label-gated) learning, directed exploration of a molecular latent space, chemistry-based filtering, and quantum-mechanical (DFT) verification within a single, reproducible pipeline.

The outcome is concrete and, we believe, of broad interest to the issue’s readership: twelve novel, DFT-confirmed leads, including HMX-class candidates obtained by a generative model and confirmed by first-principles audit. Unlike the baseline generators we evaluated on the same corpus, our approach consistently identifies molecules that are both structurally novel and competitive on the targeted performance properties. All code, model checkpoints, and validation resources are released publicly, which we hope will make the contribution easy to reproduce, extend, and cite. On these grounds we are optimistic that the paper would be a strong fit for the Special Issue and could become one of its more visible and well-cited contributions.

Abstract. The design of new high-energy-density materials is a data-limited inverse-design problem: of approximately 66,000 labelled CHNO molecules, only about 3,000 carry experimental or DFT-quality property values, so generative models trained on the full mixture either memorise the high-performance tail or extrapolate without calibration, and no new HMX-class compound has been disclosed in fifteen years. We introduce Domain-Gated Latent Diffusion (DGLD), a data-driven framework that couples generative inverse design to first-principles validation. A four-tier label-trust gate routes only high-quality labels into the conditional gradient while noisy labels train the unconditional prior; a multi-task score model supplies independently switchable sample-time steering over viability, sensitivity, and hazard; and a four-stage screening funnel (SMARTS gate, Pareto reranker, GFN2-xTB triage, and B3LYP/def2-TZVP density-functional theory) validates every candidate. DGLD yields twelve DFT-confirmed novel leads; the headline compound, 3,4,5-trinitro-1,2-isoxazole, reaches a calibrated density of 2.09 g cm−3 and a Kamlet–Jacobs detonation velocity of 8.25 km s−1 while remaining structurally distinct from all 65,980 training molecules (nearest-neighbour Tanimoto 0.27). A co-headline lead, 4-nitro-1,2,3,5-oxatriazole, reaches a calibrated detonation velocity of 9.00 km s−1 from a chemotype family distinct from that of the headline compound. Against four baselines on the same corpus, DGLD is the only method generating molecules simultaneously novel and on-target under first-principles validation. Code, model checkpoints, and mined hard negatives are released openly on Zenodo.

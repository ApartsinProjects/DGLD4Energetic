Dear Editors,

Please find enclosed our manuscript, “Domain-Gated Latent Diffusion for the Inverse Design of Novel HMX-Class Energetic Materials with First-Principles Validation,” which we submit for consideration as an original research Article in the Special Issue “AI in Materials Design and Discovery.”

The design of new high-energy-density materials is a data-limited inverse-design problem: of roughly 66,000 labelled CHNO molecules, only about 3,000 carry experimental or DFT-quality property values, and no new HMX-class compound has been disclosed in fifteen years. We introduce Domain-Gated Latent Diffusion (DGLD), a generative diffusion framework that couples inverse design to first-principles validation. A four-tier label-trust gate routes only high-quality labels into the conditional gradient while noisy labels train the unconditional prior; a multi-task score model provides independently switchable steering over viability, sensitivity, and hazard; and a four-stage screening funnel (SMARTS filtering, a Pareto reranker, GFN2-xTB triage, and B3LYP/def2-TZVP density functional theory) validates every candidate.

The principal findings we believe will interest the readership of Molecules are:

  • DGLD yields twelve DFT-confirmed novel leads in the HMX performance class, several chemically distinct from anything in the training set.
  • The headline compound, 3,4,5-trinitro-1,2-isoxazole, reaches a calibrated density of 2.09 g cm−3 and a Kamlet–Jacobs detonation velocity of 8.25 km s−1 while remaining structurally distinct from all 65,980 training molecules (nearest-neighbour Tanimoto 0.27).
  • Benchmarked against four generative baselines on the same corpus, DGLD is the only method producing molecules that are simultaneously novel and on-target under first-principles validation.
  • The label-gating recipe is domain-agnostic and transfers to any inverse-design setting that pairs abundant weak labels with a scarce trustworthy subset and a suitable validation funnel.

The work sits squarely within the scope of the Special Issue: it is a machine-learning method for materials design and discovery, demonstrated end to end on a chemically demanding target class and grounded throughout in quantum-chemical validation rather than surrogate scores alone. To support reproducibility, the code, trained model checkpoints, and datasets are released openly under a permanent DOI, and the full computational protocol is given in the Supplementary Information.

We confirm that this manuscript is original, has not been published previously, and is not under consideration for publication elsewhere. All authors have approved the submission and agree to its content. The authors declare no competing interests. The study involved no human participants, animals, or identifiable personal data, so ethical approval was not required.
